I Think It's a Buy. The AI chip designer's stock now prices in very little of next year's planned growth. By Daniel Sparks – Nov 25, 2026 at 7:43PM EST +Fool.comonShare Summarize with AI Key Points Broadcom's revenue growth sped up to 86% in its latest quarter, from 22% a year before. Management expects AI semiconductor revenue to double again in fiscal 2027. A short customer list is the biggest risk to that plan. 10 stocks we like better than Broadcom › Broadcom (AVGO +3.82%) stock fell about 5% on Wednesday to under $290 as I write, on a day when chip stocks sold off broadly. The slide took the chipmaker to its lowest point since July 2025, even below the $291.53 close it hit on Nov. 5. Shares are now about 40% under June 2's record close of $481.57. The business is going the other way. Management's September guidance was for 93% year-over-year revenue growth in its fiscal fourth quarter of 2026 (the period that ended Nov. 1, 2026). That would be an even faster rate than the quarter before. At its lowest price in over a year, is Broadcom a buy? I think so. Today, investors are paying for very little of Broadcom's artificial intelligence (AI) growth. What gives me pause is how few companies that growth relies on. Image source: Getty Images. Growth is still speeding up Broadcom's revenue growth has accelerated sharply in the last year. For its fiscal third quarter (the period that ended Aug. 2, 2026), sales climbed 86% year over year to $29.6 billion, up from 22% growth the year before. Most of the gain was from AI chips. AI semiconductor revenue more than tripled from the year before, hitting $16.7 billion, or 56% of the total. Non-GAAP (adjusted) earnings per share jumped 96% to $3.32, and free cash flow grew 95% to $13.7 billion. Management expects more. Its forecast calls for fiscal 2027 AI revenue of about $115 billion, twice its expected fiscal 2026 total. And on the September earnings call, CEO Hock Tan said Broadcom has already lined up the supply to get there. What are investors paying for? If Broadcom's fiscal fourth quarter lands where management guided (revenue of $34.8 billion with an adjusted operating margin of 66%), adjusted earnings would be roughly $3.80 per share by my math. Four quarters at that pace would total about $15 per share. Today's price, under $290, is about 19 times these earnings. The S&P 500 (^GSPC +1.60%), for comparison, traded at about 17 times forward earnings in FactSet's Nov. 20 report. Put another way, even if Broadcom's profits never grew past that quarter's pace, the stock would cost only slightly more than the market on a price-to-earnings basis. And if AI revenue doubles next year as planned, earnings should climb well past that pace. With the earnings per share analysts expect on average for fiscal 2027, the price-to-earnings ratio falls to around 15. The biggest risk is the customer list I suspect that low price-to-earnings ratio has a lot to do with how few buyers drive Broadcom's growth. The company estimates in its latest quarterly filing that its five largest end customers made up around half of its revenue in the first three quarters of fiscal 2026. "[I]n some ways, I'm so glad we have only 6 customers to deal with," Tan said on the September call, talking about the buyers of Broadcom's custom AI chips. He'd just laid out the shortages, from power to memory, that control when a customer's AI data center can come online. For shareholders, though, this short list is the main risk. As of Aug. 2, Broadcom had committed to buy around $127 billion in supplies, mainly inventory, and its filing warns that customers might cancel or delay orders over issues like a lack of capital or delayed AI build-outs. Its maximum potential liability is around $43 billion, but it thinks the chances of having to pay are low. If one big customer cuts back, Broadcom might feel it in its sales and in these commitments at the same time. Broadcom stock dropped 12% on Nov. 20. That morning, analyst Ming-Chi Kuo of TF International Securities posted that his industry survey found Google, owned by Alphabet, cutting its planned 2027 shipments of one Broadcom-designed line of its custom AI chips by around 20%. Neither company has confirmed that. But Tan said in September that four of the six customers, Google included, are financially strong enough to fund their build-outs themselves. And in April, Broadcom disclosed a long-term deal to develop and supply Google's future generations of custom AI chips. Tan said Broadcom plans to ship tens of billions of dollars in chips to Google each year for the next several years. Because investors are paying so little for next year's growth, I think Broadcom stock is a buy below $290. But with so much of fiscal 2027 riding on six customers, I'd start with a small position and build it up gradually.
