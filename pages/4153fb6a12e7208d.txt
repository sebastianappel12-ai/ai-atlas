The Dow Jones Industrial Average erased a loss of more than 1,100 points to close higher on Nov. 25, 2026, while the Nasdaq fell amid a selloff in AI stocks. Kidfly182 / Wikimedia Commons CC BY-SA 4.0 Wall Street staged one of its sharpest intraday turnarounds of the year on Wednesday, as the Dow Jones Industrial Average erased a loss of more than 1,100 points to close higher, while a broad selloff in artificial intelligence stocks, hotter inflation data and a new round of proposed tariffs left the technology-heavy Nasdaq lower for the day and on track for its worst November in nearly two decades. The Dow rose 164.25 points, or 0.33%, to finish at 50,182.95 after trading down more than 2% at its low. The S&P 500 slipped 0.25% to 7,182.78, recovering most of a decline that reached about 3.2% during the session, and the Nasdaq Composite fell 0.62% to 23,585.80 after dropping as much as 4.6%. The small-cap Russell 2000 gained 0.71%. The day's low point came in the morning, when the Nasdaq 100 briefly fell 20% below its record closing high, the common threshold for a bear market. Bargain hunters stepped in through the afternoon ahead of the Thanksgiving holiday, with most sectors turning positive by late trading and technology shares climbing well off their lows. Markets are closed Thursday for Thanksgiving and will reopen Friday for a shortened session. The rebound did little to change the month's picture. The S&P 500 is down roughly 5% in November, and both it and the Nasdaq remain on pace for their worst November since 2008, the year of the global financial crisis. Market volatility stayed elevated, with the Cboe Volatility Index, Wall Street's so-called fear gauge, rising more than 7% to about 28. FILE: The Nasdaq MarketSite tower stands in Times Square in New York City on Sept. 19, 2021. The Nasdaq 100 briefly fell 20% below its record closing high on Nov. 25, 2026, before tech stocks pared losses. ajay_suresh / Wikimedia Commons CC BY 2.0 Chipmakers and other AI-linked companies bore the brunt of the selling. Nvidia, the world's most valuable chipmaker and the bellwether of the AI trade, fell as much as 8% in early trading before closing down about 4%. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index dropped more than 6% at its low, with memory chipmakers Micron Technology and Sandisk among the hardest hit. Amazon and Alphabet each fell around 5% in morning trading, while Microsoft and Apple also declined. Dell Technologies fell more than 5% even after raising its annual revenue and profit forecasts, a sign of how little good news was being rewarded in the sector. HP Inc. was a notable exception, jumping about 10% after reporting fourth-quarter revenue of $16 billion, above analyst estimates. The slide extended a weekslong retreat driven by doubts over whether the enormous, heavily debt-financed spending on AI data centers and chips can produce returns large enough to justify it. Analysts have pointed to the pace of capital expenditure by large technology companies and signs that data center construction could slow as headwinds for the sector. "I think the market has changed; the buyers have changed," said Barbara Doran of BD8 Capital Partners, arguing that the balance of power in the market has shifted away from the investors who drove the AI rally. Software companies have been caught in the same downdraft. Shares of several major software makers, including Salesforce, Adobe, ServiceNow and Intuit, have fallen sharply this year as investors weigh whether AI tools will erode demand for their products. "Software is being hit by a double whammy of AI disruption and rates staying higher for longer," said Torsten Slok, chief economist at Apollo. Nvidia's leadership turmoil has added to the volatility. The company's reinstatement of co-founder Jensen Huang as chief executive, after his removal earlier this month, sparked a relief rally on Monday in which the S&P 500 and Nasdaq posted their biggest one-day gains since April 2025. That rally faded quickly as attention returned to the underlying questions about AI spending and to the political fallout around Huang, which has drawn scrutiny in Congress. Political risk has weighed on the sector in other ways. Investors have grown more concerned about tighter federal oversight of AI since Democrats swept the midterm elections this month, taking control of both chambers of Congress. Wednesday's economic data offered no relief. The personal consumption expenditures price index, the inflation measure the Federal Reserve watches most closely, rose 0.3% in October and was up 3.8% from a year earlier, the highest annual rate since 2023. Core PCE, which excludes volatile food and energy prices, climbed 3.3% from a year earlier, slightly above the 3.2% economists had expected. FILE: The Marriner S. Federal Reserve / Wikimedia Commons Public Domain A separate report showed the economy grew at a 4.3% annualized rate in the third quarter, well above expectations of about 3.5%. Strong growth combined with rising prices strengthened expectations that the Fed will raise interest rates rather than cut them. Futures traders were pricing in a roughly 98% chance of a quarter-point rate hike at the central bank's December meeting, according to CME Group data, with significant odds of another increase in January. Higher energy costs have been a key driver of the inflation rebound. Oil prices have climbed amid the U.S. conflict with Iran and attacks on shipping and energy infrastructure in the Persian Gulf, and U.S. crude rose about 2.6% on Wednesday to around $84 a barrel as Yemen's Houthis claimed a strike on Saudi Arabia's largest refinery. Trade policy added another layer of uncertainty. The Trump administration on Wednesday proposed new tariffs on 14 trading partners, including China, the European Union, Japan and India, accusing them of propping up domestic industries and flooding world markets with cheap goods. The proposal, which would impose duties of up to 20% and take effect no earlier than January, is the administration's latest attempt to rebuild the sweeping tariff program the Supreme Court struck down earlier this year. China faces the steepest proposed rates, on top of duties dating to President Donald Trump's first term. Nomura strategist Charlie McElligott pointed to the combination of higher rates, wider credit spreads and rising volatility, which he said was pushing investors toward cash and Treasury bonds. "It simply feels bad out there," he said, questioning what Monday's rally had actually changed. The flight to safety showed up in the bond market. The yield on the 10-year Treasury note fell to about 4.82% during the session as investors bought government debt, while gold rose about 0.5%. Bitcoin edged higher to around $60,000. Breadth was weak for much of the day. In morning trading, declining issues outnumbered advancers by about 3.5 to 1 on the New York Stock Exchange and by more than 4.5 to 1 on the Nasdaq, where 329 stocks hit new 52-week lows. The stock selloff has spilled into a broader debate about the economy's direction heading into 2027. Defenders of the AI investment cycle argue that the spending is building infrastructure that will lift productivity for years, and that the third-quarter growth figure shows the economy remains resilient. Skeptics counter that much of that growth has been fueled by the same data center boom now under scrutiny, leaving the economy exposed if companies pull back. For households, the market swings come alongside persistently high borrowing costs. The average rate on a 30-year fixed mortgage eased to 7.21% this week from 7.28% a week earlier, according to Freddie Mac, which its chief economist Sam Khater called "modest relief for prospective homebuyers." The Fed's December policy meeting is now the key event for markets. A rate increase would be the central bank's response to inflation that has moved further away from its 2% target, but it would also raise financing costs for the same capital-intensive technology companies at the center of the selloff. Before then, investors face a thin holiday-shortened trading week, with Friday's half-day session typically marked by light volume. Traders will also watch early reads on Black Friday spending for signs of how consumers are holding up under higher prices, alongside any movement on the tariff proposal and further developments at Nvidia.
