PHOTO: REUTERS THE Nasdaq Composite ended sharply lower on Thursday (Nov 5), notching its deepest one-day drop in 19 months and confirming it was in a correction, as chipmakers sank on fears of tougher AI regulation after Democrats won control of the US Congress. Democratic wins on Tuesday, including in states where opposition to new data centres has mounted, fuelled concerns that tighter regulation could slow the AI infrastructure buildout that has helped lift stocks to record highs this year. Nvidia and other major chipmakers tumbled. “Investors appear to be recognising that some newly elected members of Congress and governors are looking to slow the pace of AI infrastructure development,” said Melissa Brown, global head of investment decision research at SimCorp. “While it seems unlikely that they will be able to stop it altogether, any slowdown would raise concerns because the AI growth story has largely depended on the rapid buildout of supporting infrastructure.” The Nasdaq Composite logged its biggest daily drop since President Donald Trump’s “Liberation Day” tariff announcement in April 2025. The index ended down more than 10 per cent from its Oct 6 record-high close, confirming a technical correction. Meta Platforms, Amazon.com and Alphabet fell. Data centre operators IREN and Applied Digital, neoclouds CoreWeave and Nebius Group all tumbled. AI-exposed utilities and equipment suppliers GE Vernova and Constellation Energy also dropped. Elon Musk’s Tesla and SpaceX both dropped. Musk spent nearly half a billion dollars backing Republican candidates in the midterm elections. The Nasdaq declined 5.52 per cent to 24,315.00 points, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 3.44 per cent to 51,523.06 points. Following Thursday’s selloff, the S&P 500 remains up 7.4 per cent in 2026, while the Nasdaq is up 4.6 per cent. Volume on US exchanges was very heavy, with 26.9 billion shares traded, compared to an average of 17.7 billion shares over the previous 20 sessions. On Wednesday, the S&P 500 clocked its biggest one-day percentage gain in three months, as political uncertainty eased, boosting risk appetite. Some analysts said a divided government in Washington could lead to fewer sharp legislative changes and may make it harder to agree on fiscal spending. Cleveland Federal Reserve President Beth Hammack and Richmond Fed chief Thomas Barkin hinted at the possibility of future rate hikes in comments on Thursday. Focus will be on the US October nonfarm payrolls data due on Friday. Traders currently expect the Fed to hike rates next month following the September rate hike.
