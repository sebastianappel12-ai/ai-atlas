Nachman warned that input costs remain high for businesses on both the labor and fuel side, which is impacting the local advertising market. Nachman indicated that some consumers are choosing to "repair versus replace" for larger home service tasks, stating that "some of those bigger projects are not getting done." SUMMARY Yelp Inc. (NYSE:YELP) reported a 1% year-over-year revenue decline in the third quarter as record growth in data licensing and AI products was offset by macro headwinds in its core advertising segments. Management noted that high borrowing costs and persistent inflation have dampened consumer demand for large-scale home projects and restaurant visits. The company is actively shifting its investment profile toward AI-driven offerings like Yelp Host and Hatch while leveraging automated engineering tools to improve internal productivity. Although the company executed $200 million in share repurchases during the first nine months of the year, it paused the program in the third quarter to prioritize the repayment of its revolving credit facility. Stoppelman noted, "personal AI agents have gained considerable momentum in recent months," which has increased the demand for Yelp's structured local data. The company expects to recognize a tax benefit of approximately $30 million in the fourth quarter related to previously unrecognized tax benefits. Nachman reported that food ordering volume within the Yelp Host product grew sixfold from June to October. Stoppelman indicated that Request-a-Quote functionality is now fully integrated within ChatGPT through a partnership with OpenAI. Management reported that food order conversion rates increased by 13% for a major restaurant client following the deployment of new voice AI solutions. Schwarzbach stated that the company plans to return more than 50% of free cash flow to shareholders annually through share repurchases starting in 2027. INDUSTRY GLOSSARY ARR: Annual run rate revenue, a metric used to project future revenue based on current performance. CPC: Cost-per-click, the amount an advertiser pays for each click on their advertisement. Hatch: An acquired AI-powered lead management platform for home service professionals. PALs: Paying advertising locations, representing the number of unique business locations with active advertising. RR&O: Restaurants, Retail, and Other, a Yelp reporting segment focusing on non-service businesses. SBC: Stock-based compensation, a non-cash expense used to attract and retain employees. Yelp Assistant: A conversational AI tool that helps consumers find businesses and submit project requests. Yelp Host: An AI-powered call answering service designed for restaurants to handle reservations and food orders.
