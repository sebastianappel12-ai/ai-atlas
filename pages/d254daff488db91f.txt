Market Mastery Webinar by Vishal Malkan Find the weak links in your portfolio by Vishal Malkan Register for FREE Webinar Register for FREE US stock market crash: Nvidia, Tesla, SpaceX and other technology giants suffered massive losses in market value on Thursday, as Wall Street witnessed its steepest selloff in more than a year. Nvidia alone shed around $440 billion in market capitalisation, while Tesla and SpaceX together lost approximately $420 billion, as fears of tighter AI regulation following the US midterm elections triggered a sharp reversal in technology stocks. The selloff wiped out as much as $1 trillion in combined market value from the Magnificent Seven technology stocks, while companies in the S&P 500 collectively lost approximately $3 trillion in market capitalisation. The Nasdaq Composite plunged 5.52 percent to 24,315, its biggest one-day decline in 19 months, confirming a technical correction after falling more than 10 percent from its October 6 record closing high. The S&P 500 dropped 4.36 percent to 7,353.73, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 3.44 percent to 51,523.06. US stocks and bonds rise, dollar falls after Trump's midterm loss Nvidia, the world's leading supplier of AI accelerators, was among the biggest casualties, with its shares falling more than 9 percent and erasing approximately $440 billion in market capitalisation. Tesla and SpaceX also suffered sharp declines, with losses of up to 9 percent each and a combined erosion of around $420 billion in market value. Other major technology companies were caught in the selling pressure. Meta Platforms declined 4 percent, while Amazon and Alphabet, Google's parent company, each fell 2.2 percent. The semiconductor sector witnessed even sharper losses. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index plunged more than 12 percent, reflecting widespread selling in companies supplying chips and equipment for artificial intelligence infrastructure. Qualcomm fell 9.5 percent after the chipmaker's fourth-quarter smartphone chip sales missed Wall Street expectations, adding company-specific pressure to the broader technology selloff. AI data centre stocks suffer double-digit losses Companies directly exposed to the rapid expansion of AI data centres were among the worst affected. IREN and Applied Digital plunged more than 14 percent each, while CoreWeave dropped almost 10 percent, as investors questioned the outlook for large-scale AI infrastructure investments. The weakness extended to companies supplying electricity and equipment for data centres. GE Vernova declined 7.5 percent, while Constellation Energy fell 2.5 percent. Why did Big Tech stocks crash after the US midterm elections? The selloff followed the Democrats' sweeping victory in the US midterm elections, which gave the party control of both chambers of Congress. Democratic victories in states where opposition to new data centres has intensified raised concerns that tougher regulation could delay construction projects and slow the pace of AI infrastructure investment. These worries compounded existing questions over the returns on the enormous sums being invested in artificial intelligence. The technology rout also spread to Asian markets on Friday. South Korea's KOSPI plunged as much as 8.8 percent, triggering a circuit breaker, while Japan's Nikkei dropped more than 5 percent as semiconductor stocks continued to face heavy selling.
