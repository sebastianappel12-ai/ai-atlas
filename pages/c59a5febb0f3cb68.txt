By Tony Jackson Published 11/06/26 AT 8:10 PM AEDT Share on Facebook Share on Twitter Share on LinkedIn Share on Reddit Share on Flipboard Share on Pocket Sims Shares Drop 7% Amid Global AI Rout as Recycler With Data Center Hardware Arm Heads Toward November AGM SYDNEY - Shares of Sims Ltd. fell more than 7% on Friday as a global selloff in technology and artificial intelligence stocks hit the Australian market, weighing on the metals recycler, which has built a growing business around data center hardware. Sims shares lost A$1.53, or 7.22%, to close at A$19.65, after trading between A$19.29 and A$20.95. About 883,000 shares changed hands, leaving the company valued at roughly A$3.8 billion. The ASX 200 closed 0.5% lower as the AI selloff that began on Wall Street spread across Asia. Sims did not release market-sensitive news on Friday. Data center exposure The company has increasingly tied its growth to AI infrastructure. Sims Lifecycle Services posted 91% growth in repurposed units and is positioning for GPU repurposing. That business was a key driver of last year's results. Underlying EBIT surged 170% to A$468 million, led by Sims Lifecycle Services growth and strong non-ferrous pricing. The near-term outlook for that unit is softer. The company expects Sims Lifecycle Services EBIT of A$75 million to A$90 million in the first half of fiscal 2027, with lower near-term volumes of high-speed DDR4 memory. Recent results reaction Investors were already cautious. Sims shares fell 12.78% to A$22.52 from A$25.82 after its full-year update in August. Return on invested capital reached 11.7%, a key milestone for shareholder returns. The company paid a fully franked final dividend of 20 cents a share, bringing its full-year dividend to 34 cents. AGM ahead Shareholders will soon have a chance to question management. Sims scheduled its 2026 annual general meeting for 9 a.m. on Friday, Nov. 20, as a hybrid meeting in Sydney. Shareholders will vote on granting CEO and Managing Director Stephen Mikkelsen 117,300 performance rights under the Long Term Incentive Plan.
