Key Points Warren Buffett hung up his work coat for the final time at the end of last year, paving the way for Greg Abel to take the reins and overhaul Berkshire Hathaway’s portfolio. Several of the Oracle of Omaha’s core and “indefinite” holdings remain pillars of Berkshire’s $362 billion investment portfolio. However, an artificial intelligence (AI) powerhouse and an energy duo are also playing key roles. Motley Fool Issues Rare "Total Conviction" Buy Alert › It's been a year of historic change for Berkshire Hathaway (NYSE:BRKA) (NYSE:BRKB) and its shareholders. Longtime CEO Warren Buffett retired at the end of last year, handing the reins to the company's day-to-day operations and its closely followed $362 billion investment portfolio to Greg Abel. But while some things have changed -- Abel dumped 16 of Berkshire's non-core positions in the first quarter -- one core principle has remained the same: portfolio concentration. As of the end of October, seven standout stocks accounted for nearly 71% ($255.2 billion) of Berkshire's portfolio: Missed AI’s "Act 1"? Act 2 Could Be 15x Bigger. Most investors think they missed the AI boat because they didn't buy Nvidia in 2005. But according to our analysts, we’re only at the end of "Act 1"—the R&D phase. "Act 2" is the global rollout. Coca-Cola and Amex are the two longest-held stocks in Berkshire's portfolio (since 1988 and 1991, respectively), and two of the companies Buffett referred to as "indefinite" holdings in his 2023 annual letter to shareholders. Aside from both companies possessing well-defined competitive advantages, they're generating jaw-dropping yields of 65% for Coca-Cola and 45% for Amex, relative to Berkshire's cost basis for each company. Though Bank of America isn't guaranteed to stick around under Abel's leadership, its share price premium to book value has notably declined over the last two months, and it's ideally positioned to take advantage of a rising interest-rate environment. Image source: Getty Images. There's a new "Apple" of Abel's eye Even though the Oracle of Omaha sold 75% of iPhone maker Apple's shares in the nine quarters leading up to his retirement, it remains the undisputed No. 1 holding by market value. Aside from Apple's market-leading $879 billion in cumulative share repurchases since the start of 2013, Abel appreciates its innovative capacity and believes it can be a multidecade compounder. But since taking the reins in January, Abel has found a new "Apple" of his eye. While Buffett initiated Berkshire's stake in Google's parent Alphabet, it's Abel who's been relentlessly building this position through the first half of 2026. Although Alphabet possesses the sustainable moat that both Buffett and Abel love -- Google accounts for about 90% of global internet traffic -- it's the integration of artificial intelligence (AI) solutions within Google Cloud that has Berkshire's new boss excited. The substantially higher-margin Google Cloud more than doubled sales in the latest quarter. Advertisement Energy is a core theme Lastly, energy remains a core theme, with Chevron and Occidental Petroleum collectively accounting for more than 10% of Berkshire Hathaway's invested assets. While both positions were entered well before Abel became CEO, don't forget that Abel spent several years as CEO of MidAmerican Energy (later renamed Berkshire Hathaway Energy). He has a keen understanding of the global energy market and the growing demand for energy solutions in the wake of the AI data center build-out.
