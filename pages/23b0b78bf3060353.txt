Published November 17, 2026 By Doug Burger , Technical Fellow and Corporate Vice President, Microsoft Research Ava Amini , Principal Research Manager Eric Horvitz , Chief Scientific Officer Dr. Eric Topol , Executive Vice President, Scripps Research Share this page Share on Facebook Share on X Share on LinkedIn Share on Reddit Subscribe to our RSS feed Video playback requires cookie consent Technical advancement is moving at such a rapid pace that it can be challenging to define the tomorrow we’re working toward. In The Shape of Things to Come, Microsoft Research leader Doug Burger and experts from across disciplines tease out the thorniest AI issues facing technologists, policymakers, business decisionmakers, and other stakeholders today. The goal: to amplify the shared understanding needed to build a future in which the AI transition is a net positive. Microsoft Principal Research Manager Ava Amini, Microsoft Chief Scientific Officer Eric Horvitz, and physician-scientist and Microsoft AI health advisor Eric Topol (opens in new tab) of Scripps Research join Burger to tackle a question with ramifications for every individual on the planet: What might the future of medicine look like with AI, and what needs to happen first? Drawing from experiences in machine learning, bioscience, and clinical care, the group explores AI-assisted medical diagnosis, the untapped potential of leveraging AI in medical imaging, whole-patient models, virtual cells, and the role of biological foundation models in scientific discovery. The group also examines why promising research can be slow to reach patients, how AI could guide experiments and drug discovery, and why better biological data and real-world validation remain essential. This episode delivers a research-grounded conversation of what AI can do today in the health space, the challenges and opportunities still ahead, and the shape of things to come.
