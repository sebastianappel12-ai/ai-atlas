Anthropic View Price & Profile Scottish Mortgage NAV total return rises 31.5% as SpaceX listing and Moderna trial success lift performance Published: 02:40 06 Nov 2026 EST Scottish Mortgage Investment Trust PLC (LSE:SMT), managed by Baillie Gifford, delivered a net asset value (NAV) total return of 31.5% in the six months to 30 September, against 16.0% for the FTSE All-World Index. The share price total return was 33.8%, and shareholders’ funds, the trust’s net assets, rose to £17.69 billion from £13.82 billion at 31 March. SpaceX, the rocket maker that listed on 12 June, was the largest contributor, adding 8.0 percentage points, and the trust reduced its stake to 15.0% of total assets by 30 September and 11.1% by 4 November. Moderna, the biotechnology company, added 4.5 percentage points after its shares nearly tripled in a day in August when a personalised cancer vaccine succeeded in a late-stage melanoma trial. Anthropic, the artificial intelligence developer, contributed 3.4 percentage points, while Chinese holdings PDD, BYD and CATL each fell more than 24%. The trust declared an interim dividend of 1.80p a share, up 12.5% from 1.60p, extending 43 consecutive years of annual increases. Net gearing, a measure of borrowing relative to assets, fell to 4.3% from 11.0%, reflecting portfolio gains and the repayment of borrowings, partly funded by reductions in its SpaceX holding. It reissued 40.95 million shares from treasury at a premium, raising approximately £602 million net of costs, then bought back 72.9 million shares for approximately £1.06 billion before transaction costs after they returned to a discount. It sold its remaining Tesla stake, which returned 13 times the capital invested since 2013, and bought shares in chip suppliers and equipment makers including SK hynix, KLA and DISCO. Over five years, however, NAV gained 27.8% against 79.3% for the index, while the 10-year gain of 463.6% compared with 228.4%. The shares stood at 1,590.5p on 30 September, representing a discount to NAV of 7.9%, compared with 9.5% at 31 March.
